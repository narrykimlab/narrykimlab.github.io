# Research

RNA lies at the core of gene regulation across all biological systems,
from viruses to humans. We explore the fundamental principles of RNA
regulation and apply these insights to develop next-generation RNA-based
therapeutics, asking how regulatory RNAs and their associated factors
shape cellular behavior and how they can be harnessed for therapeutic
innovation.

<section class="research-section" markdown="1">

## mRNA Stability, Tailing, and Modification

{% picture figure-section images/research/mrna-stability.jpg alt="A diagram: a poly(A)+ mRNA is deadenylated rapidly and its body decays, unless TENT4A or TENT4B adds a mixed tail (AGAA), which CNOT6L or CNOT7 deadenylates only with delay" class="qb-fig-right qb-fig-section" %}

RNA tails play key roles in RNA function and stability. Building on our
discovery of 3′ uridylation and adenylation in microRNAs, we developed
TAIL-seq to profile mRNA tails at a genome-wide scale and uncovered
noncanonical tailing pathways such as uridylation and mixed tailing. We
are dissecting the mechanisms of RNA tail modification to understand how
they stabilize mRNAs and how this knowledge can enhance mRNA therapeutics.

## Viral Regulatory RNAs and mRNA Therapeutics

{% picture figure-section images/research/viral-rna.jpg alt="The folded structure of a viral RNA element, with its G bulge deletion (K5m), above a tree of the Picornaviridae in which the kobuviruses, human Aichi virus among them, are marked" class="qb-fig-right qb-fig-section" %}

Viruses encode powerful RNA elements that modulate translation and RNA
stability. Using high-throughput, sequencing-based screens across
thousands of viral genomes, we identify viral RNAs with potent regulatory
activities and investigate how they remodel host pathways. These studies
reveal new modes of RNA regulation and offer design principles for RNA
therapeutics and vaccines.

</section>

## Cellular Regulation of Exogenous RNAs

Cells mount distinct responses to foreign RNAs—including viral RNAs and
therapeutic mRNAs. We examine how cells recognize, modify, defend against,
or cooperate with exogenous RNAs at the molecular level. This work
provides a framework for improving the stability, translation, and safety
of therapeutic mRNAs.

## microRNA Biogenesis and Function

{% picture figure-section images/research/rna-protein-structure.jpg alt="A ribbon model of proteins bound to an RNA hairpin" class="qb-fig-right qb-fig-section" %}

microRNAs are essential regulators of development, physiology, and
disease. Our lab has elucidated key steps in microRNA biogenesis by
identifying core factors such as DROSHA, DGCR8, LIN28, and TUTases, and by
determining their structures. We continue to investigate how microRNAs are
produced and regulated.
