V. Narry Kim is an SNU Distinguished Professor in the School of Biological
Sciences at Seoul National University and the Director of the
[Center for RNA Research](https://rna.ibs.re.kr) at the Institute for Basic
Science (IBS). She studies RNA-mediated gene regulation: how regulatory RNAs
and the proteins that act on them control gene expression, and how that
knowledge can be put to work in RNA-based therapeutics.

Her group worked out how microRNAs are made, identifying core factors of
microRNA biogenesis such as DROSHA, DGCR8, LIN28 and the TUTases, and
determining their structures. Building on its discovery of 3′ uridylation
and adenylation in microRNAs, the group developed TAIL-seq to profile mRNA
tails across the genome and uncovered noncanonical tailing such as
uridylation and mixed tailing. More recently it has mapped the
[SARS-CoV-2 transcriptome](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.04.011)
and screened thousands of viral genomes for RNA elements that control
translation and RNA stability, work that now feeds into the design of mRNA
therapeutics.

She studied Microbiology at Seoul National University and received her
Ph.D. in biochemistry from the University of Oxford in 1998. After
postdoctoral training as a fellow of the Howard Hughes Medical Institute at
the University of Pennsylvania, she returned to Seoul National University in
2001 and has led her laboratory there since.

She is a Foreign Member of the Royal Society, a Foreign Associate of the US
National Academy of Sciences and of EMBO, and a member of the Korean Academy
of Science and Technology. Her honors include the L’Oréal-UNESCO Women in
Science Award (2008), the Ho-Am Prize in Medicine (2009), the HUGO Chen
Award (2017), the Asan Award in Medicine (2019), the Lim Sung Ki
Researcher Award (2024), the Changjo Medal of the Order of Science and
Technology Merit (2026), and the Human Frontier Science Program Nakasone
Award (2027). She serves on the Board of Reviewing Editors of
*Science* and on the editorial boards of *Cell*, *Molecular Cell*,
*Genes & Development* and *The EMBO Journal*.
